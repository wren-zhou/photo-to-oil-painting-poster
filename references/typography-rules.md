# Typography and mask
The artwork uses one continuous painted scene, not separate images inside each letter. Conceptually render a scene, mask the upper portion by a huge headline against ivory, reveal the full scene below, and composite selected foreground silhouettes in front. Letter counters and inter-letter gaps remain ivory in the upper masked area. The lower headline joins the landscape without a rigid horizontal seam. The sky/scene inside the letters must align with the lower artwork.

- Subtitle: top center, approximately 3–9% of canvas height, small thin charcoal handwritten cursive, natural informal strokes, no decorative flourish overload.
- Headline: single line, ultra-bold tall condensed sans-serif uppercase (Impact/Anton-like visual character, not a mandatory font), approximately 90–96% canvas width, begins around 10–14% of height, occupies roughly 35–50% of height, adaptable by scene. Avoid cropped letters and overly tight counters.
- Selected trees, mountains, architecture or landmarks may overlap letters and enter ivory gaps. Maintain legibility; avoid covering distinguishing glyph features.
- Only exact headline and subtitle. Prefer 4–7 letters and 3–7 subtitle words; shorter or longer may be used if appropriate. Avoid redundant copy, unsupported place names, extra slogans and invented dates.
- No solid-color title, outline letters, text boxes, separate photo tiles, drop shadows or visible mask seams.

For exact commercial typography, an optional external compositing workflow can render a text-free painting, use an editable vector text mask, and composite foreground alpha masks. This skill does not implement that compositor or guarantee print resolution.
