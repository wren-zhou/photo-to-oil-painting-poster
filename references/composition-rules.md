# Scene-adaptive composition
Choose by visual structure, not merely subject labels. All strategies retain key source geometry and favor natural extension to a vertical canvas.

| Strategy | Use for | Mask and overlap |
|---|---|---|
| Botanical framing | Flower-lined streets, forests | Branches and leaves overlap letter edges from sides; sky remains visible in letter interiors; preserve street perspective. |
| Mountain silhouette | Alpine, hills, open land | Preserve mountain silhouette; peaks interrupt lower glyphs; painted meadow and forest extend below. |
| Landmark breakthrough | Tall landmarks, people with monuments | Landmark crosses headline vertically; preserve recognizable proportions, keep people and pose intact in lower field. |
| Urban perspective | Streets, architecture | Maintain vanishing point, building façades, and street activity; buildings overlap lower sides of headline. Optional modest foliage only if compatible with source. |
| Horizon integration | Beaches, lakes, water | Horizon and distant vegetation merge with lower headline; shore curve and foreground frame remain intact. |
| Adaptive | Other subjects | Identify a natural continuous background for letter fill and a recognizable foreground occluder. If no sky exists, use scene-appropriate background rather than inventing unrelated sky. |

When landscape photo becomes portrait, preserve the primary subject before optimizing the title. Do not squeeze the photo. Use selective extension of sky/foreground and restrained repositioning. No new prominent landmarks or altered human anatomy.
